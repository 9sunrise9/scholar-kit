Abstract—In this article, we present an adaptive reinforcement learning optimal tracking control (RLOTC) algorithm for an underactuated surface vessel subject to modeling uncertainties and time-varying external disturbances. By integrating backstepping technique with the optimized control design, we show that the desired optimal tracking performance of vessel control is guaranteed due to the fact that the virtual and actual control inputs are designed as optimized solutions of every subsystem.
To enhance the robustness of vessel control systems, we employ neural network (NN) approximators to approximate uncertain vessel dynamics and present adaptive control technique to estimate the upper boundedness of external disturbances. Under the reinforcement learning framework, we construct actor–critic networks to solve the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations corresponding to subsystems of surface vessel to achieve the optimized control. The optimized control algorithm can synchronously train the adaptive parameters not only for actor–critic networks but also for NN approximators and adaptive control. By Lyapunov stability theorem, we show that the RLOTC algorithm can ensure the semiglobal uniform ultimate boundedness of the closedloop systems. Compared with the existing reinforcement learning control results, the presented RLOTC algorithm can compensate for uncertain vessel dynamics and unknown disturbances, and obtain the optimized control performance by considering optimization in every backstepping design. Simulation studies on an underactuated surface vessel are given to illustrate the effectiveness of the RLOTC algorithm.
Index Terms—Actor–critic networks, adaptive optimal tracking control, reinforcement learning, underactuated surface vessel.

## I. INTRODUCTION
O VER the past decades, tracking control of marine vessels has received great attention in ocean engineering due to its theoretical challenges [1], [2] and many important applications including ocean rescue operation, resource exploration, environmental monitoring, and military purpose. In practical applications, many vessel control systems are underactuated because they only provide the control forces for surge and yaw motions [3]. Many important theoretical results for tracking control of underactuated surface vessels are available in the literature. By Lyapunov’s direct approach and backstepping technique, asymptotic tracking control was presented in [4] for underactuated vessels. When there exist uncertain parameters in system model, robust adaptive control was developed to solve the point-to-point navigation problem for underactuated vessels under the passive-boundedness condition [5]. By the learning capability of radial basis function (RBF) neural networks (NNs) [6], adaptive NN-based output feedback control was proposed in [7] for uncertain underactuated vessels. In [8], an adaptive NN control design technique for underactuated vessels was developed to guarantee the prescribed performance of tracking errors. However, the above-mentioned control design techniques do not consider the optimization issue, and the control efforts may consume massive energy.
Optimal control is a fundamental design principle, and it can be applied to not only solve the tracking control problem but also optimize the control performance of dynamic systems. Since ocean transportation or deep-sea exploration often requires massive energy supply, it is very necessary to consider the optimization in the vessel control design to reduce the energy consumption. For a nonlinear control system, in general, the optimal controller is expected to obtain by solving Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation.
However, it is difﬁcult or impossible to obtain an analytical solution of the HJB equation since it is generally a nonlinear partial differential equation. To tackle the intractable problem, Bellman [9] initially proposed a dynamic programming (DP) method to achieve the optimal control inputs.
An adaptive/approximate DP (ADP) [10] was presented to deal with the well-known “curse of dimensionality” [9]. So far, many optimal control techniques have been proposed for nonlinear systems [11], [12]. Based on ADP algorithm, robust optimal control design was developed in [13] for nonlinear systems in the presence of dynamic uncertainties. Compared with the existing work [26], where NN-based observer was designed to identify completely unknown terms including uncertain system dynamics and disturbances, the presented optimal control algorithm can improve the robustness of underactuated surface vessels and obtain the better control performance. This is because the number of neurons used for NN approximators is much less than those used for completely unknown systems and the disturbances are not taken as NN inputs in this work. Additionally, dynamic surface control (DSC) technique [31], [32], [33] is embedded in the presented actor–critic RL algorithm to avoid the derivative of virtual controls in the backstepping procedure, which can yield a concise weight updating law and reduce the computational complexity. By incorporating backstepping technique with the actor–critic RL algorithm, the optimal tracking control problem for an underactuated surface vessel is solved in the sense of totally optimal policy and cost functions. We perform two comparative simulations with adaptive NN control (ANNC) [8] and the existing RL strategy [23]. Simulation results show that the proposed RLOTC algorithm has faster convergence rate and higher tracking accuracy. It is worth pointing out that the presented actor–critic RL algorithm is a powerful technique to determine the optimal control solution online, which not only optimizes the control action but also evaluates the control performance. Therefore, the RLOTC strategy becomes a preferable alternative for enhancing the optimized tracking performance of underactuated vessels.

Compared with the case that vessel’s model is accurately known a priori, the parameter uncertainties could result in slower damped oscillations and larger tracking errors in the dynamic response of adaptive control systems [25]. In [26] and [27], two novel RL-based optimal tracking control schemes were developed for fully actuated surface vessels in presence of complex unknown dynamics.
In this article, we study the RL-based optimal tracking control (RLOTC) problem for an underactuated surface vessel with modeling uncertainties and time-varying external disturbances. Although backstepping procedure is extensively presented to design tracking controllers for underactuated vessel systems [4], [5], [7], [8], it is still a challenging issue for integrating backstepping procedure with optimization into the vessel control design due to the underactuation and coupled vessel dynamics. Different from the existing tracking control of surface vessels [8], [28], [29], the major difference of this work is the consideration of optimization in the vessel control design. Remark 2: The actor–critic architecture is the one of the most widely used strategies for performing RL algorithm [47], where the actor performs the control behaviors by interacting with its environment, and the critic evaluates the behaviors and then sends the feedback to the actor for improving the subsequent control behaviors. Under the actor–critic learning architecture, the critic and actor NNs are, respectively, employed to approximate the optimal value functions and the optimal control policy for learning of the solution of HJB equation. The use of a single-critic NN architecture [37], [38], [39] instead of actor–critic NN learning algorithm can offer the opportunity of reduced computational burden. Compared with the single-critic NN architecture, the actor–critic learning algorithm could ﬁnd more suitable approximations of the optimal value functions and the optimal control policy by tuning the critic and actor weight update laws, respectively, and could yield optimal control efforts. Therefore, the presented actor–critic RL algorithm is a ﬂexible and powerful technique to determine the optimal control solution online, which not only optimizes the control action but also evaluates the control performance.

## V. CONCLUSION

Vessel position (x, y) tracks the reference trajectory (xd, yd) for RLOTC.
This article developed an online RLOTC algorithm for an underactuated surface vessel in the presence of modeling uncertainties and time-varying disturbances. To perform the RL strategy, an actor–critic learning architecture was employed, where the actor NNs and critic NNs were used to approximate the optimal control policy and the optimal value functions, respectively, and their weight values can be simultaneously adjusted online. Meanwhile, NN approximators were constructed to approximate uncertain vessel dynamics, and adaptive control technique was presented to estimate the upper bounds of the disturbances. Based on NN approximation, DSC technique, backstepping procedure, and Lyapunov synthesis, adaptive optimal tracking controllers were designed such that tracking errors can converge to a small neighborhood of zero in the optimal sense and all signals of the closed-loop systems are SGUUB.
