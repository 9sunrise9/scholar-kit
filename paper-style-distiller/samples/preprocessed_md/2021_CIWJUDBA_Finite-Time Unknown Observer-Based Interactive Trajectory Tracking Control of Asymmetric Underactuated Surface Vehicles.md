Abstract—In this brief, a ﬁnite-time unknown observer-based interactive trajectory tracking control (FUO-ITTC) scheme is created for an asymmetric underactuated surface vehicle (AUSV).
In lieu of a selﬁsh trajectory which might be essentially inaccessible for an AUSV, the philosophy of an interactive trajectory, which features freely prescribed surge and yaw dynamics, and sway interactions with cross-tracking dynamics, is innovatively established for the ﬁrst time, and thereby contributing to a family of uniformly trackable trajectories which completely remove persistent-excitation constraints on the desired yaw rate. By deﬁning a composite coordinate transformation (CCT), interactive trajectory tracking error dynamics are formulated in a cascade structure with ﬁnite-time vanishing residuals. By virtue of the CCT-based cascade structure, FUO-based surge and yaw controllers are separately synthesized with an interaction law.
The entire FUO-ITTC closed-loop system stability is guaranteed by combining the Lyapunov approach with cascade analysis, which in turn rigorously proves that both kinematic and dynamic tracking errors globally asymptotically converge to zero.
Comprehensive experiments and comparisons are conducted on a benchmark AUSV and demonstrate the remarkable performance of the proposed FUO-ITTC scheme in terms of both transient and steady-state tracking accuracy.
Index Terms—Asymmetric underactuated surface vehicle (AUSV), composite coordinate transformation (CCT), ﬁnite-time unknown observer (FUO), interactive trajectory tracking.

Note that nonholonomic systems with nonintegrable velocity constraints, e.g., mobile robots [14], can be transformed into a chained form and can be handled by a backstepping-like recursive technique [15]. However, nonintegrable acceleration constraint appears in sway dynamics of the USV such that a driftless chained system cannot be obtained by any coordinate transformation [16]. Subsequently, using a global diffeomorphism in [15], Pettersen and Nijmeijer [17] transformed the USV tracking error dynamics into a triangularlike form whereby the yaw velocity was taken as an additional virtual control input, thereby enabling the backstepping technique. Note that the desired yaw dynamics are assumed to be tracked exactly all the time. In this context, recursive approaches for an entire chained system become unavailable for trajectory tracking control of the USV.
In the early stage, to effectively address the underactuation issue on an USV, the mass and damping matrices are usually simpliﬁed to be diagonal [17], [18] such that the entire tracking error dynamics can be transformed into a cascaded form consisting of two subsystems, i.e., translational and rotational dynamics [20], [21]. In this context, tracking control of an USV can be formulated in the cascaded framework whereby two nominal subsystems are connected by a coupled nonlinearity. It has been proven in [21] that the entire cascaded system is globally uniformly asymptotically stable (GUAS) if its solutions are globally uniformly bounded (GUB) and nominal subsystems are GUAS. Similarly, results on global K-exponential stability were derived from the work [22]. By exploiting uniform complete controllability (UCC) of a linear timevarying (LTV) system, global K-exponential tracking control of an USV has been developed in [23]. Note that it is rather complicated to test the UCC condition pertaining to the USV translational dynamics. Thanks to a persistent excitation (PE) condition, the UCC validation was avoided in [24], whereby a combined cascade-backstepping (CB) approach has been proposed. In addition, by virtue of passivity properties within the USV tracking error dynamics, a passivity approach has also been addressed in [24]. By employing a new transformation [25], the USV tracking error dynamics have been nested into a novel cascaded structure which can be stabilized with K-exponential convergence under a much weaker PE condition. Similarly, global asymptotic and exponential results have been obtained in [26] by combining the transformation in [18] with backstepping technique. Note that the PE condition on desired yaw dynamics makes aforementioned approaches unavailable for straight-line trajectory tracking.

Subsequently, various results have been extended to complex cases including output feedback [28], input and state constraints [29], and parametric uncertainties [30]. Recently, sliding mode control (SMC) approach has also been deployed to deal with unknown hydrodynamics and disturbances [31]. However, it should be noted that all aforementioned methods require the USV be semi-submerged sphere, i.e., both port-starboard and fore-aft symmetry, since the mass and damping matrices are assumed to be diagonal. In practice, an USV would signiﬁcantly violate the diagonal assumption due to fore-aft geometric asymmetry and complex hydrodynamics, and thereby making a suitable cascaded structure cannot be derived from previous coordinate transformations.
Recently, increasing attention has been paid to nondiagonal inertia and/or damping [32] pertaining to an USV. By introducing a translation along the surge direction within the body-ﬁxed frame, the inertia matrix can be changed to a diagonal form. In this context, transformed sway dynamics can be converted to be independent on yaw control moment, simultaneously. However, it still requires the inertia matrix be symmetric [34], [35], which would be signiﬁcantly violated in high-speed situations [35, pp. 69]. Nevertheless, comparing to fully actuated marine vehicles [36]–[41] which can be directly tackled by adaptive intelligent approximationbased approaches [42]–[44] and/or disturbance observer-based control approaches [45]–[47], complex unknowns including unmodeled dynamics, external disturbances in addition to uncertainties pertaining to an USV can hardly be addressed within cascaded tracking error subsystems since the involved unknown terms would make the cascade analysis unavailable.
Note that ﬁnite-time unknown observers (FUOs) can exactly identify unknowns within a short time, and have attracted great efforts by virtue of homogeneity analysis [48]–[50] and high-order sliding mode [51]–[54]. Accordingly, an FUO can be deployed to separate unknown observation from control synthesis, and thereby achieving accurate rejection to unknowns.
In view of the foregoing literature review, one can observe that trajectory tracking problem of an USV with asymmetric inertia masses and/or damping hydrodynamics remains largely open, in addition that complex unknowns including unmodeled dynamics, disturbances and/or uncertainties cannot be handled in the current cascade framework. In this context, we attempt to create a promising solution by addressing the following unresolved issues.
1) Inspired by the seminal work [32], a composite coordinate transformation (CCT) is devised to establish onestep technique for decoupling trajectory tracking error dynamics whereby yaw control moment on sway tracking error dynamics can be completely removed. As a consequence, yaw control law can be synthesized in an independent manner.
2) To facilitate cascade analysis on the entire USV trajectory tracking error dynamics, the FUO is designed by employing high-order sliding mode technique and achieves exact observations on complex unknowns.

## D. Performance Comparisons
Furthermore, in order to quantitatively demonstrate the superiority of the proposed FUO-ITTC framework, comprehensive comparisons on both transient and steady-state trajectory tracking performance measured by integral absolute error (IAE) and integral time-weighted absolute error (ITAE) indices, respectively, are conducted in this section. Note that the proposed FUO-ITTC scheme would degrade to the so-called cascade-backstepping (CB) method [24] when both the interactive dynamics and the FUO technique are removed from the proposed framework. In this context, fair comparisons to the typical CB approach together with its FUO-based variant (i.e., FUO-CB) can be made by sharing the common user-deﬁned parameters used in Section V-A–V-C. Actually, by virtue of the proposed CCT technique, the CB method is made applicable to the AUSV since the original CB approach cannot deal with asymmetric inertia masses. Performance comparison results for nonzero constant, zero and asymptotic DYRs are summarized in Tables III–V, respectively, from which we can see that the proposed FUO-ITTC approach achieves the highest trajectory tracking accuracy in terms of both IAE and ITAE measurements, while the FUO-CB and CB methods are signiﬁcantly inferior to both FUO-ITTC and ITTC (whereby the FUO is not used) schemes in all cases. In this context, the superiority of ITTC schemes can be clearly demonstrated in terms of both transient and steady-state performance. In addition, we can observe that the FUO deﬁnitely plays a key role in enhancing trajectory tracking performance by exactly identifying complex unknowns, regardless of the ITTC and CB approaches.
## VI. CONCLUSION
In this brief, tracking of an AUSV suffering from complex unknowns to a family of accessible trajectories has been solved by creating an FUO-ITTC scheme. A CCT tool has been proposed to decouple asymmetric sway and yaw accelerations, and thereby facilitating the DYR and the yaw control synthesis, simultaneously. By virtue of devising FUOs, complex unknowns pertaining to the AUSV can be exactly observed within a short time. In this context, surge and yaw controls have been synthesized in a novel cascade framework. In addition, using cross-tracking errors, interactive sway behaviors have been elaborately executed. As a consequence, a set of trackable trajectories have been dynamically created to remove underactuated constraints on the AUSV. Meanwhile, the previously required PE condition on the DYR has been completely removed. Comprehensive comparisons have been conducted to demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed FUO-ITTC framework. In the future work, interactive formation of the AUSV would be a practically interesting trend.
